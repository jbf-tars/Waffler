    Email signature

    • Clean-up takes out ums and false starts, adds punctuation and tidies the sentence. With it off, Waffler pastes exactly what it heard, with your words and Snippets. Nothing is sent for clean-up. Styles aren't used, but an email signature still is.
    • Applies to new dictations. Works in English and other Latin-script languages.
    • Automatic: Waffler writes as it does today. Nothing is changed. Formal: capital letters and full stops, as Waffler writes them. Casual: takes the full stop off a one-sentence message. Very casual: lower case, and no last full stop. Names in your words and acronyms keep their capitals. Excited: ends short statements with an exclamation mark.
    • Your signature is added in Mail, Outlook and other email apps, when you start with a greeting or say ten words or more. Where the app allows it, Waffler looks at the text before your cursor, only to check your signature isn't already there. It isn't kept or logged.

        • On this computer: your speech is turned into text on this computer, after one download. No key, and it works with the internet off.
        • Your own key: your speech is sent to Groq or OpenAI with the key you add. Quicker, especially for long meetings.
        • This choice covers dictation and meetings. Writing a meeting’s notes still needs a key, either way.
        • The writing model is for an Apple Silicon Mac with 16 GB of memory or more: about two seconds to tidy a dictation, a minute or two for a long meeting’s notes. A key is quicker, and better with long meetings.
        • Waffler checks a key with its provider before saving it. Groq keys start with gsk_, OpenAI keys with sk-, Cerebras keys with csk-.

            • Everything stays on this computer, except what you say while you hold the keys, and the half-second before. That goes straight to the providers you set up, on your own key.
            • Journal. Your dictations, searchable in your Journal. Each notes the app you were in, the language and which services handled it. The app is kept with its kind (email, work chat, personal chat or other), on this computer only. Each also notes how loud your speech arrived, so Waffler can tell you if your microphone is set too low. If you correct a dictation with Edit text, your corrected version is kept with it, on this computer only, for as long as the dictation is.
            • Export the Journal. Save your Journal as a file on your Desktop. If iCloud or OneDrive backs up your Desktop, it backs this file up too. It never includes your keys. It's your own copy, so Delete all my data leaves it alone. Your words and Snippets export from Words, the same way.
            • Recent recordings. The audio of your last 10 dictations (fewer if they are very long, about 300 MB at most), so you can redo a dictation, get one back if Waffler stops, or look into a problem. Off, new recordings are not kept, so a dictation can't be redone or recovered.
            • Transforms. Only when you use a transform, Waffler reads the text you selected, with Accessibility or by copying it, and sends it to your provider on your own key. The text before and after is kept in your Journal, and Delete all my data clears it with the rest.
            • Command mode. Only while you hold your Command shortcut, Waffler records what you say, turns it into text with your speech provider, and sends that with the text you selected (or up to 500 characters before your cursor) to your provider on your own key. A question opens your browser with only your spoken question. The recording is not kept. What you said and the text before and after are kept in your Journal, and Delete all my data clears them with the rest.
            • Tips seen. Which tips Waffler has shown, and which you hid, so it doesn't repeat them. Counts and times only.
            • Microphones seen. Which new microphones Waffler has asked you about, and what you answered, so it asks once for each. Names and answers only, and Delete all my data clears them.
            • Microphone volume. When your dictations keep arriving very quiet, Waffler reads your microphone's input volume at most once a day, to suggest turning it up, and on Windows its name, so the tip can say which microphone. It never changes it. The reading and when it was taken stay on this computer, and Delete all my data clears them.
            • Logs. A zip on your Desktop with no keys, no transcripts and no signature text. Handy for a bug report.
            • Delete all my data. Your Journal, your meetings (notes, transcripts and audio), usage, recordings, logs and which tips you've seen. Your keys, words, snippets and settings stay.

            While it pastes

            • Your clipboard. Unless you keep your text there, Waffler holds what you had copied in memory for about a second, then puts it back. It never stores or logs it.
            • The text field you paste into. To confirm a paste landed, Waffler reads whether that field changed. It never stores or logs what is in it.
            • The text you selected. Only when you use a transform: Waffler reads your selection, through Accessibility or by copying it and putting your clipboard back, and sends it to your provider on your own key. It never reads a password field.
            • The text near your cursor (Command mode). Only while you hold your Command shortcut with nothing selected: Waffler reads up to 500 characters before your cursor, to give your command some context, and sends them to your provider on your own key. It never reads a password field.
            • The text before your cursor. Only if you turn on an email signature, in an email app: Waffler reads the text before your cursor to check your signature isn't already there. It never stores or logs it.
